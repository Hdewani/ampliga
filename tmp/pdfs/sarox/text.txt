The  Challenge  
Electrical  testing  generates  a  large  amount  of  technical  information.  For  every  job,  technicians  
may
 
need
 
to:
 
●  Enter  customer  and  equipment  information.  ●  Record  readings  from  insulation  and  LCR  testing  instruments.  ●  Organize  multiple  test  runs.  ●  Calculate  diagnostic  ratios  and  corrected  values.  ●  Compare  measurements  across  electrical  phases.  ●  Identify  abnormal  imbalance.  ●  Build  charts  and  summary  tables.  ●  Assign  condition  ratings.  ●  Format  the  final  customer  report.  
When  these  activities  are  distributed  across  instrument  displays,  handwritten  notes,  
spreadsheets,
 
and
 
document
 
templates,
 
the
 
same
 
information
 
may
 
be
 
handled
 
several

That  creates  four  business  problems.

Repetitive  work

or

A  typical  manual  reporting  workflow  would  involve:  
Create  a  job  record          ↓  Write  down  equipment  information          ↓  Capture  readings  from  testing  instruments          ↓  Transfer  readings  into  a  spreadsheet          ↓  Check  and  organize  the  data          ↓  Calculate  test  ratios  and  phase  imbalance          ↓  Create  charts  and  summary  tables          ↓  Copy  the  results  into  a  report  template          ↓  Review  and  correct  the  report          ↓

Save  and  deliver  the  final  document

The  Objective  
The  application  needed  to  achieve  six  practical  objectives:  
1.  Create  one  reliable  record  for  customer,  project,  equipment,  and  test  information.  2.  Reduce  repeated  data  entry  by  reusing  stored  information  throughout  the  workflow.  3.  Apply  calculations  consistently  across  the  application,  PDF  reports,  and  Excel  
workbooks.
 4.  Make  technical  results  easier  to  review  through  summaries,  charts,  condition  ratings,  
and
 
polar
 
plots.
 5.  Generate  professional  reports  quickly  without  rebuilding  them  manually.  6.  Preserve  technician  control  over  testing,  interpretation,  review,  and  final  report  
approval.
 
The  application  also  needed  to  work  locally  because  electrical  testing  may  take  place  in  
environments
 
without
 
dependable
 
internet

Our  Approach  
We  organized  the  application  around  the  technician’s  real  working  sequence.  
First,  we  separated  the  workflow  into  three  categories.  
Steps  suitable  for  automation  
●  Reusing  project  and  equipment  information  ●  Storing  readings  ●  Formatting  electrical  values  ●  Calculating  ratios  and  imbalance  ●  Applying  approved  rating  rules  ●  Creating  charts  
===== PAGE 5 =====
●  Building  report  tables  ●  Generating  PDF  and  Excel  files  
Steps  requiring  technician  control  
●  Connecting  and  configuring  test  instruments  ●  Confirming  equipment  details  ●  Selecting  the  correct  test  ●  Reviewing  unusual  readings  ●  Interpreting  exceptions  ●  Approving  the  final  report  
Steps  requiring  operational  safeguards  
●  Detecting  incomplete  or  invalid  information  ●  Handling  overloaded  measurements  ●  Preventing  inconsistent  report  values  ●  Preserving  records  between  sessions  ●  Managing  instrument  connection  failures  ●  Allowing  reports  to  be  reviewed  before  export  
This  approach  automated  repetitive  processing  without  removing  professional  judgment  from  
the
 
technician.

Technical  Architecture  
 
The  application  does  not  require  a  cloud  platform  or  external  CRM  to  complete  its  core  workflow.  
 
How  the  Automation  Works  
Step  1:  A  test  record  is  created  
Trigger:  The  technician  selects  “New  Record.”  
What  happens:  The  application  creates  a  unique  record  for  the  job.  
System  involved:  React  desktop  interface  and  local  SQL  database.  
What  happens  next:  Customer,  project,  equipment,  and  testing  information  can  be  added  to  
the
 
record.
 
Step  2:  Equipment  information  is  captured  
Trigger:  The  technician  enters  project  and  equipment  details.  
What  happens:  Information  such  as  the  client,  equipment  configuration,  testing  location,  
insulation
 
class,
 
wire
 
markings,
 
and
 
test
 
voltages
 
is
 
saved.
 

===== PAGE 7 =====
System  involved:  Information  form  and  local  database.  
What  happens  next:  The  information  becomes  available  to  the  test  screens  and  report  
generator
 
without
 
being
 
entered
 
again.
 
Step  3:  Insulation  readings  are  collected  
Trigger:  The  technician  starts  an  insulation  test  or  receives  readings  from  the  supported  
instrument
 
workflow.
 
What  happens:  Time,  applied  voltage,  actual  voltage,  current,  and  resistance  values  are  
recorded
 
against
 
the
 
selected
 
test
 
run.
 
System  involved:  Insulation-testing  interface,  serial  communication  layer,  and  database.  
What  happens  next:  The  data  becomes  available  for  calculation,  graphing,  and  reporting.  
Step  4:  Winding  measurements  are  recorded  
Trigger:  The  technician  selects  a  multimeter  field  or  sends  a  supported  measurement  
command.
 
What  happens:  The  application  stores  the  measured  value  together  with  relevant  temperature  
and
 
frequency
 
information.
 
System  involved:  Multimeter  interface,  serial  communication  layer,  and  database.  
What  happens  next:  Phase  measurements  can  be  compared  and  used  in  report  summaries.  
Step  5:  Diagnostic  values  are  calculated  
Trigger:  Sufficient  test  data  becomes  available.  
What  happens:  The  application  calculates  relevant  results,  including  Polarization  Index,  
Dielectric
 
Absorption
 
Ratio,
 
corrected
 
insulation
 
resistance,
 
and
 
phase
 
imbalance.
 
It  also  assigns  consistent  condition  bands:  
●  Excellent  ●  Good  ●  Normal  ●  Observe  ●  Caution  ●  Alarm  
System  involved:  Application  calculation  and  rating  logic.

What  happens  next:  Results  appear  in  the  report  overview  with  the  related  tables  and  charts.  
Step  6:  The  technician  reviews  the  report  
Trigger:  The  technician  opens  the  Report  screen.  
What  happens:  The  application  retrieves  the  equipment  information,  insulation  results,  winding  
measurements,
 
calculations,
 
ratings,
 
and

System  involved:  Report  overview  and  local  database.  
What  happens  next:  The  technician  can  check  the  report  before  creating  the  final  file.  
Step  7:  The  report  is  exported  
Trigger:  The  technician  selects  “Export  PDF”  or  “Export  Excel.”  
What  happens:  The  application  formats  the  available  data,  calculations,  charts,  ratings,  and  
branding
 
into
 
the
 
selected
 
report
 
format.
 
System  involved:  PDFKit  for  PDF  generation  and  ExcelJS  for  Excel  generation.  
What  happens  next:  A  save  dialog  allows  the  technician  to  choose  the  final  file  location.

Technical  Challenges  
Challenge  1:  Maintaining  consistent  results  across  three  outputs  
The  same  measurement  appears  on  the  report  screen,  in  the  PDF  report,  and  in  the  Excel  
workbook.
 
If

results

could
 
receive

Solution:  Calculation,  formatting,  and  rating  rules  were  standardized  across  the  reporting  
workflow.
 
This
 
includes
 
resistance

phase
 
imbalance,
 
temperature
 
correction,
 
test
 
ratios,
 
and
 
condition

Challenge  2:  Presenting  complex  electrical  data  clearly  
Raw  electrical  readings  are  necessary  for  technical  review,  but  they  are  difficult  for  many

and
 
business

to

quickly.
